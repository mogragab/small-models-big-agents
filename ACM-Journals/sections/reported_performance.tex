\section{The Public Evaluation Record for Small Language Models}
\label{sec:reported}

This section examines what the public evaluation record can support about \gls{slm} capability. The values discussed are \emph{reported} figures from vendor technical reports, model cards, community leaderboards, and third-party studies. We set out the cohort, sources, and grading scheme (Section~\ref{subsec:reported_protocol}), quantify why a cross-model table cannot be assembled from vendor reporting (Section~\ref{subsec:reported_overall}), and explain why these benchmarks do not answer the agentic question in any case (Section~\ref{subsec:reported_agentic_gap}).

\subsection{Cohort, Sources, and Grading}
\label{subsec:reported_protocol}

\subsubsection{Cohort and Sources}
\label{subsec:reported_model_selection}
\label{subsec:reported_sources}
The cohort was chosen purposively: 18 models with public weights or an evaluation card, below 10B parameters (Phi-4 at 14B retained as an upper-bound reference), at least one score on an established benchmark, release no later than March 2025 so that at least eighteen months of third-party evaluation had accumulated by the snapshot, and a spread of training methods (Table~\ref{tab:model_specs} in \suppsec~\ref{supp:tables}). It spans 0.5B to 14B and eight developers, and it contains none of the 2025--2026 hybrid, reasoning, or mobile-multimodal families, so the audit describes reporting practice for 2023--2025 releases. Values were sought from vendor technical reports, then official model cards, then single-harness community evaluations such as HELM \citep{liang2023helm} and the Open \gls{llm} Leaderboard \citep{hf2024openllmlb}, then peer-reviewed third-party studies. A reported number is comparable with another only if five conditions match: benchmark version, prompt template, answer-extraction rule, decoding settings, and harness \citep{biderman2024lessons}; \suppsec~\ref{supp:audit} defines each.

\subsubsection{Evidence Grading}
\label{subsec:reported_grading}

Because this survey mixes controlled studies, vendor self-reports, independent evaluations, and our own synthesis, each empirical claim carries a grade. The grade is set by two properties of the strongest source behind the claim: its \emph{design} (a controlled comparison, in which the compared conditions differ only in the variable of interest under one harness with a matched baseline; a single-harness evaluation of many systems; a self-report; or an estimate or synthesis) and its \emph{independence} (whether the reporting party has a stake in the result).

\begin{description}
  \item[\evA{} Controlled, independent] A controlled comparison reported by a party without a stake in the outcome.
  \item[\evB{} Independent] Any other evaluation or documented count by a party without a stake: a single-harness evaluation across models, an independent benchmark, or a reproduction.
  \item[\evC{} Reported] Stated by the developer of the artifact or the author of the technique, including developer-run comparisons on a single harness.
  \item[\evD{} Interpretation] Our synthesis across sources, an inference from evidence gathered for another question, or an estimate in a position paper.
\end{description}

Table~\ref{tab:claim_grades} grades the principal claims, and Tables~\ref{tab:compression_quality} and~\ref{tab:agentic_evidence} grade the technique and agent evidence. A grade-\evC{} claim is unverified rather than false; where vendor reports and independent reproductions of the same model exist, the vendor figure is usually the more favorable, so the likely direction of error is known.

\subsection{Why a Common Benchmark Table Cannot Be Built from Vendor Reporting}
\label{subsec:reported_overall}

We originally intended a single table placing the cohort side by side on a shared benchmark set, assembled from the vendors' documents. The vendor record cannot support it, and we report the measured reasons instead. The released coverage ledger (\file{audit\_coverage\_ledger.csv}; \suppsec~\ref{subsec:audit_ledger}) records, for the 14 cohort models from Microsoft, Alibaba, Meta, and Google, whether each model's primary release document reports an instruction-tuned value on each of the eight benchmarks that recur in their headline reporting (\gls{mmlu}, MMLU-Pro, ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, \gls{gsm8k}, MATH, HumanEval, MBPP), and under which stated protocol.

\emph{Vendors do not report a common benchmark set.} Of the 112 model--benchmark cells, 69 carry an instruction-tuned value, and no benchmark is reported for all 14 models; the best covered, \gls{gsm8k} and MATH, are reported for 11.

\emph{Where they report the same benchmark, they rarely use the same protocol.} Only 45 of the 69 reported cells state a protocol at all; the Qwen2.5 report, for example, gives no shot counts for its instruction-tuned tables. Among stated protocols, no benchmark has a protocol shared by more than five of the 14 models: \gls{mmlu} is 5-shot for Phi and Llama~3.2 and 0-shot for Gemma~3; \gls{gsm8k} is 8-shot with chain-of-thought for Phi and Llama~3.2 and 0-shot for Gemma~3; ARC-Challenge is 10-shot for Phi and 0-shot for Llama~3.2. Shot count alone moves multiple-choice accuracy by several points \citep{biderman2024lessons}, so a column headed by one protocol would be false for most of its cells. This finding needs no rule beyond protocol matching.

\emph{Pre-trained and instruction-tuned checkpoints are reported separately.} For 10 of the 14 models, the vendor tabulates the pre-trained and the instruction-tuned checkpoint in different tables under different protocols, and secondary sources often copy a figure without its checkpoint. For Gemma~3-4B, the technical report gives \gls{gsm8k} 38.4 for the pre-trained checkpoint (8-shot) and 89.2 for the instruction-tuned one (0-shot with chain-of-thought), and MMLU-Pro 29.2 and 43.6 respectively \citep{team2025gemma3}; a row mixing the two misstates a gap by tens of points.

\emph{Matched comparisons exist but are vendor-run.} Some vendors publish single-harness comparison tables: Microsoft compares Phi-4-mini with ten other models, five of them in this cohort, on an internal platform that keeps prompts and few-shot examples identical across models \citep{microsoft2025phi4minicard}, and Meta reports Llama~3.2 1B and 3B at BF16 beside their quantized versions on the same harness \citep{meta2024llama32card}. These meet the harness condition but are run by an interested party that also chose the comparators and benchmarks, so they are grade~\evC{} evidence and cannot be merged across vendors.

The general point, that scores are not comparable across evaluation setups, is the premise of HELM, the Open \gls{llm} Leaderboard, and the reproducibility analysis of \citet{biderman2024lessons}; the contribution here is to quantify it for the \gls{slm} cohort. Single-harness third-party evaluations are the right basis for comparison: the Open \gls{llm} Leaderboard v2 ran every submission through one configuration on six benchmarks \citep{hf2024openllmlb}, \citet{lu2024slmsurvey} evaluate 70 models of 0.1--5B under one setup including on-device latency, and \citet{srivastava2025thinkslm} evaluate 72 models under one reasoning protocol. None covers the whole cohort, and their benchmark sets differ from the vendors', so they replace the vendor table rather than filling it in. What can be said from the vendor record without distortion is qualitative (\evD): the strongest 3--4B models of 2024--2025 report scores that 13B-class models of 2023 did not, and no single \gls{slm} leads across all task families.

\subsection{Why These Benchmarks Do Not Answer the Agentic Question}
\label{subsec:reported_agentic_gap}

Every benchmark above measures a model answering a question in isolation. An agent asks a model to select one of several tools, to emit arguments that satisfy a schema, to stay well formed across a long tail of inputs, to recover when a tool returns an error, and to do so repeatedly. A model can score well on \gls{mmlu} and still emit malformed calls often enough to be unusable in a loop, and a small model tuned for function calling can score poorly on \gls{mmlu} and be adequate as an executor. Even an internally consistent version of this record would therefore be the wrong evidence for H, and Section~\ref{subsec:agentic_evidence} assembles agent-specific evidence instead.

\subsection{Quantization Impact}
\label{subsec:reported_quantization_summary}

Four points summarize the compression evidence for the cohort's size range (Table~\ref{tab:compression_quality}): INT8 post-training quantization is reported as effectively lossless (\evC); at 4 bits, reasoning is largely preserved in an independent benchmark of 72 small models, while methods tuned on large models transfer imperfectly to sub-1B models (\evB); \gls{awq} is reported to retain more accuracy than GPTQ (\evC); and post-training quantization preserves fidelity better than post-training pruning at 0.5--3.8B (\evA). Per-model degradation figures are not reported consistently enough to tabulate. The memory consequence is arithmetic: 4-bit formats store 4.4--5.8 effective bits per weight, so weights shrink $2.8$--$3.6\times$ rather than $4\times$; Qwen2.5-7B falls from 15.2~GB to 4.2--4.5~GB (Table~\ref{tab:memory_footprint} in \suppsec~\ref{subsec:reported_quantization}).

\subsection{Multilingual and Safety Reporting}
\label{subsec:reported_safety}

Multilingual and safety reporting is sparser than benchmark reporting and not comparable across models. Models whose reports document large non-English pretraining mixtures, such as Qwen2.5 \citep{qwen2024qwen25}, Gemma~3 \citep{team2025gemma3}, and Aya~23 8B \citep{aryabumi2024aya23}, report stronger non-English performance than English-centric families (\evC). Per-model toxicity evaluations are often absent for research-grade small models, alignment can be eroded by fine-tuning \citep{qi2023finetuning}, and compression degrades performance unevenly across languages \citep{blodgett2020language,kurz2025pruning_vs_quant}. An agent that routes non-English or safety-sensitive calls to a small English-centric model inherits these gaps, which argues for capability-aware rather than size-only routing.

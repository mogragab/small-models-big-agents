\section{A Faceted Taxonomy of Small Language Models}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

\subsection{Why the Scheme Is Faceted and Multi-Label}
\label{subsec:taxonomy_scheme}

We organize the \gls{slm} design space along four \emph{primary} facets,
\emph{architecture}, \emph{training strategy}, \emph{modality}, and
\emph{application domain} (Figure~\ref{fig:taxonomy}), and five
\emph{secondary} facets that govern whether a model can actually be adopted
(Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy_facets}). Two properties of the scheme need stating up
front, because earlier presentations of this taxonomy, including our own, got
them wrong.

First, the facets are \emph{not orthogonal}, and it would be misleading to
present them as such. Training strategy and architecture are entangled: the
pruning-plus-distillation route presupposes a dense teacher, and the
mixture-of-experts route interacts with distillation through which experts are
supervised. Modality and architecture are entangled through the encoder a
vision-language model attaches. What the facets are is \emph{separately
observable}: each can be read off a model's primary source independently of the
others, which is what makes them usable as an indexing scheme even though they
covary.

Second, the scheme is \emph{multi-label}, not a partition. A model may occupy
several categories on one facet at once. Gemma~3 is distilled \emph{and} trained
on a curated corpus from scratch; Granite~4.0 is a hybrid state-space model
\emph{and} a mixture of experts; Qwen2.5-7B is used as a general-purpose model
\emph{and} as a multilingual and a code model. Forcing one label per facet, as a
partition would, produces exactly the misclassifications a reader would then have
to undo. In the inventory each variant therefore carries a set of labels per
facet, and the counts in the figures that follow are counts of labels, not of
models, except where stated.

The application-domain facet needs a further caveat. Unlike the other three, it
is usually a statement about \emph{how a model is used} rather than an intrinsic
property of the artifact: a general-purpose 7B model fine-tuned by a hospital on
clinical notes is a healthcare model in deployment and a general-purpose model on
the Hub. We therefore assign a domain label only when the developer trained or
tuned the released artifact for that domain and says so in the primary source,
and we treat the facet as the weakest of the four.

\subsection{Taxonomy by Architecture}
\label{subsec:taxonomy_architecture}

\subsubsection{Dense Transformer Models}

The majority of production \glspl{slm} use a dense transformer decoder: every token attends to every other token (within window) and every parameter is activated per forward pass. This category spans from sub-1B models (SmolLM2-135M \citep{allal2025smollm2}) to 7--8B flagships (Mistral 7B \citep{jiang2023mistral}, Llama 3.1 8B \citep{grattafiori2024llama3}). Recent dense families are notable for pushing capability per parameter: SmolLM3-3B \citep{smollm3} uses grouped-query attention with NoPE and is trained on 11.2T tokens with a documented three-stage curriculum, matching or exceeding Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-3B; Qwen3's dense line \citep{qwen3_2025} spans 0.6B--32B under a single architecture combining \gls{gqa}, \gls{rope}, and SwiGLU with a unified thinking / non-thinking switch. Key architectural improvements over the vanilla GPT-2 design include:

\begin{description}
    \item[Grouped-query attention] Introduced in Llama 2 \citep{touvron2023llama2} and adopted by Mistral 7B and most post-2023 \glspl{slm}, \gls{gqa} reduces the \gls{kv} cache memory by a factor of $G$ (query heads divided by \gls{kv} heads), enabling longer context windows without a proportional memory cost.

    \item[Extended \gls{rope} base] Phi-3, Phi-4, Gemma~3, and Llama~3.2 all use \gls{rope} with extended base frequencies ($\theta = 500{,}000$--$1{,}000{,}000$) to support context windows up to 128K tokens without architectural modification.

    \item[SwiGLU feed-forward network] Replacing the standard two-layer \gls{ffn} with a Swish-gated linear unit (SwiGLU) variant, adopted across nearly all post-2022 \glspl{slm}, improves perplexity at equal parameter count and has become the default choice in every major family surveyed here.
\end{description}

These changes, together with \gls{rope} base extension and positional interpolation, have driven a rapid expansion of context windows in the \gls{slm} inventory (Figure~\ref{fig:context_by_year}): median supported context grew from a few thousand tokens in 2023 to over 100K by 2025.

\subsubsection{Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) SLMs}
\glsunset{moe}

MoE architectures \citep{shazeer2017moe,fedus2022switch} decouple \emph{total} parameters from \emph{active} parameters per token: only a subset of ``expert'' feed-forward network (FFN) blocks is activated by a learned router. This allows a model to have a large total parameter count while keeping per-token FLOPs comparable to a much smaller dense model.

Mixtral~$8{\times}7$B \citep{jiang2024mixtral} (47B total, 12.9B active per token) is the canonical example, competitive with 70B dense models while activating only about a quarter of its parameters per token. More recent \gls{moe} models in this band include DeepSeek-MoE \citep{dai2024deepseekmoe} (finer-grained experts, 2B active from 16B total) and Qwen3-30B-A3B \citep{qwen3_2025}, which activates 3B of 30B parameters and reportedly matches Qwen2.5's dense base models at a fraction of the active parameter count. Meta's Llama~4 Scout \citep{llama4_2025} (109B total, 17B active, 16 experts) applies the same principle at a much larger total scale, with Behemoth serving as the distillation teacher.

These models sit precisely on the fault line that condition C2 of
Section~\ref{subsubsec:intro_def_operative} is there to mark. Per-token latency,
FLOPs, and energy track \emph{active} parameters, so Qwen3-30B-A3B costs roughly
what a 3B dense model costs to generate a token. Download size, disk footprint,
and resident memory track \emph{total} parameters, so the same model needs on the
order of 17~GB of weight storage at 4~bits and cannot be resident on a phone.
Both facts are true at once, and a survey that reports only the first has
described a model that nobody can deploy on the hardware the first fact makes it
sound suitable for. In this paper, therefore, Mixtral~$8{\times}7$B,
Qwen3-30B-A3B, and Llama~4 Scout are \emph{small-active-parameter \gls{moe}}
models, not \glspl{slm}: they appear in the inventory and in the architectural
discussion, and they are excluded from every statement about on-device
feasibility, storage, and resident memory. Models such as Granite~4.0 Tiny
($\sim$7B total, $\sim$1B active) satisfy both conditions and are \glspl{slm}
without qualification.

Dense models still dominate the inventory numerically (Figure~\ref{fig:moe_vs_dense}). The 13 inventory entries reporting a distinct active-parameter count carry a much larger median total-parameter count at comparable per-token cost, and 6 of them exceed the total-parameter threshold.

\subsubsection{Hybrid SSM-Transformer Models}

A nascent but rapidly growing class of \glspl{slm} combines attention-based transformer layers with \glspl{ssm} \citep{gu2023mamba}, which process sequences in linear rather than quadratic time. Mamba \citep{gu2023mamba} introduced hardware-aware selective \glspl{ssm}; Jamba \citep{lieber2024jamba} interleaves transformer and Mamba blocks in a 1:7 ratio, achieving competitive performance with $2.6\times$ lower KV-cache memory than a pure transformer. In 2025--2026 this design moved from research to production at the small scale. Falcon-H1 \citep{falconh1_2025} spans 0.5B--34B with a parallel Mamba-2/attention block. IBM's Granite~4.0 \citep{granite4_2025} interleaves self-attention and Mamba-2 layers at roughly a 1:9 ratio and pairs it with a fine-grained \gls{moe}, reporting more than 70\% memory reduction for long-context and multi-session inference relative to transformer-only models, with ``Tiny'' ($\sim$7B total, $\sim$1B active) and ``Micro'' variants aimed explicitly at edge and local use. NVIDIA's Nemotron Nano~2 \citep{nvidia2025nemotron_nano2} replaces most self-attention in a 9--12B model with Mamba-2 layers (6 attention, 28 Mamba-2, 28 FFN across 62 layers) and reports up to 6$\times$ higher throughput than Qwen3-8B when generating long reasoning traces. Alibaba's Qwen3.5 small series adopts a Gated DeltaNet hybrid at a 3:1 linear-to-full-attention ratio \citep{qwen3_5_2026}. Linear-time inference is most valuable exactly where \glspl{slm} are deployed, in long-context, streaming, memory-bound edge settings, which is why the hybrid category is growing fastest.

\subsection{Taxonomy by Training Strategy}
\label{subsec:taxonomy_training}

\subsubsection{Trained from Scratch}

Most commercially significant \glspl{slm} are pre-trained from scratch on carefully curated datasets. The Phi family exemplifies a data-centric philosophy: rather than increasing parameters, Microsoft researchers found that training on ``textbook-quality'' synthetic and curated web data yields disproportionate gains \citep{gunasekar2023phi1,li2023phi2}. Phi-1 (1.3B) \citep{gunasekar2023phi1} trained on $<$8B tokens, two orders of magnitude less than contemporaries, yet achieved competitive Python coding benchmarks. Llama 3.2 3B \citep{grattafiori2024llama3} trained on 9T tokens from Meta's curated corpus. Gemma 3 \citep{team2025gemma3} scales its token budget with model size rather than applying one figure across the family, from 2T tokens for the 1B variant to 14T for the 27B, under a curriculum that progressively increases the ratio of math, code, and multilingual data.

Key training innovations in this category include: (a)~\emph{data quality filtering} using heuristic and classifier-based pipelines; (b)~\emph{synthetic data generation} from larger teacher models \citep{tinystories2023,ho2022orca}; (c)~\emph{curriculum scheduling} where token mixture evolves over training to emphasize harder reasoning data in later stages; and (d)~\emph{long-context extension} via continued pre-training with positional interpolation or extended \gls{rope}.

\subsubsection{Distilled SLMs}

Knowledge distillation \citep{hinton2015distilling} transfers capability from a large teacher model to a smaller student. In the \gls{llm} era, distillation takes several forms. \emph{White-box distillation} accesses the teacher's internal representations (intermediate hidden states, attention maps), enabling feature-level alignment. \emph{Black-box distillation} uses only the teacher's outputs (token logits or generated text) as supervision. Orca \citep{ho2022orca} demonstrated that distilling reasoning \emph{traces} from GPT-4 rather than just answers, enables a 13B student to acquire substantially stronger chain-of-thought capabilities. Reasoning distillation became a dominant 2025 strategy: DeepSeek-R1-Distill models \citep{deepseek2025r1} transfer long chain-of-thought from DeepSeek-R1 into Qwen-2.5 students at 1.5B--14B, and Phi-4-mini-reasoning \citep{xu2025phi4mini_reasoning} combines distillation from a large reasoning teacher with reinforcement learning to bring competition-mathematics performance to a 3.8B model. Gemma~3 and Gemma~4 \citep{team2025gemma3,gemma4_2026} distill every model size, including the largest, from a bigger teacher by default.

\subsubsection{Pruned and Compressed SLMs}

Rather than training a small model from scratch, pruning removes parameters from a large pre-trained model. NVIDIA's Minitron pipeline \citep{minitron2024} first prunes Llama 3.1 8B by removing attention heads, MLP neurons, and layers guided by activation statistics, then distills from the unpruned teacher, achieving a 40$\times$ reduction in training compute relative to training from scratch while matching or exceeding the performance of same-size models trained from scratch.

\subsection{Taxonomy by Modality}
\label{subsec:taxonomy_modality}

\subsubsection{Text-Only SLMs}

Text-only models still dominate the inventory (Figure~\ref{fig:modality}), processing tokenized text as both input and output. This category encompasses all major \gls{slm} families: Phi, Gemma, LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen, Yi, InternLM, SmolLM, MiniCPM, OLMo, Pythia, and DeepSeek.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{paper_slm_modality_distribution.pdf}
  \caption{Input modalities supported by documented SLM variants (a variant may support several). Vision has become common; audio and video remain a small but growing minority.}
  \Description{Bar chart of the number of documented small language model variants supporting each input modality. Text dominates, vision is common, and audio and video remain small but growing categories.}
  \label{fig:modality}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{Multimodal (Vision-Language) SLMs}

A growing subset accepts image inputs alongside text, enabling visual question answering, image captioning, document understanding, and visual reasoning. Notable examples include:

\begin{description}
    \item[PaliGemma 3B \citep{chen2023paligemma}] Google's 3B vision-language \gls{slm} combining a SigLIP-400M image encoder with a Gemma decoder; its release paper reports strong transfer to a wide set of downstream vision-language tasks after fine-tuning.

    \item[Phi-3-vision (4.2B)] Microsoft's first vision-capable Phi model \citep{abdin2024phi3}, adding a CLIP-family image encoder to Phi-3-mini's text backbone and targeting document and chart understanding.

    \item[Gemma 3 (4B/12B/27B)] Google's Gemma 3 attaches a custom SigLIP vision encoder, operating on fixed $896 \times 896$ inputs with a Pan-and-Scan cropping scheme for other aspect ratios, to the 4B, 12B, and 27B variants \citep{team2025gemma3}. The 1B variant is text-only and has a 32K rather than a 128K context window, so vision support in this family begins at 4B and is not, as is sometimes reported, present at every size.

    \item[Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B] Alibaba's multimodal \glspl{slm} \citep{qwen2024qwen25}, with native support for video, charts, and documents.
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Audio and Multi-Modal SLMs}

A smaller but rapidly growing category incorporates audio alongside text and vision. Gemma~3n \citep{google2025gemma3n} is built mobile-first: its MatFormer (``Matryoshka'') architecture nests a 2B-effective sub-model inside a 4B-effective model so that one download yields a spectrum of sizes, and Per-Layer Embeddings cut the resident memory footprint of the 5B/8B raw models to roughly that of a 2B/4B model (2--3~GB). Gemma~4 \citep{gemma4_2026} extends the family from 2.3B-effective to 31B with dense and \gls{moe} variants, an integrated thinking mode, and, for the 12B model, an encoder-free architecture that ingests raw audio and image patches directly. Apple's on-device Foundation Model \citep{gunter2025afm} is a $\sim$3B multimodal model quantized with 2-bit quantization-aware training and KV-cache sharing. The full text+vision+audio frontier in a single sub-10B model is now a shipping capability rather than a research aspiration.

\subsection{Taxonomy by Application Domain}
\label{subsec:taxonomy_domain}

\subsubsection{General-Purpose SLMs}

General-purpose \glspl{slm} are trained on diverse data and evaluated on multi-domain benchmarks (\gls{mmlu}, HellaSwag, ARC, \gls{gsm8k}). The majority of \glspl{slm} in our taxonomy, particularly the flagship families from Microsoft (Phi), Google (Gemma), Meta (LLaMA), Mistral AI, Alibaba (Qwen), and DeepSeek, fall into this category.

\subsubsection{Domain-Specific SLMs}

Domain-specific \glspl{slm} sacrifice breadth for depth, outperforming general-purpose models on narrow tasks:

\begin{description}
    \item[Healthcare] Meerkat (7B) \citep{kim2025meerkat}, fine-tuned on synthesized clinical reasoning traces, was the first 7B model to clear the USMLE pass threshold (70.6\% on MedQA) while running on a single A100 GPU; its 70B sibling edges GPT-4 on average across six medical-licensing exams. OptimCLM \citep{hasan2024optimclm} applies combined compression (distillation + quantization + pruning) to clinical language models without sacrificing diagnostic accuracy.

    \item[Code] Code Llama \citep{roziere2023codellama}, StarCoder2 \citep{lozhkov2024starcoder2}, and DeepSeek-Coder \citep{zhu2024deepseek_coder} represent a mature class of code-focused \glspl{slm} with strong HumanEval and MBPP performance.

    \item[Legal] Domain-specific legal \glspl{slm} trained on corpora of statutory text, case law, and contracts target high-volume routine work such as clause classification, obligation extraction, and regulatory change triage, where on-premises deployment satisfies privilege and data-residency constraints that API-based \glspl{llm} cannot. Public evaluation in this domain remains sparse relative to the healthcare and code verticals.

    \item[Multilingual] Cohere's Aya line illustrates a breadth-versus-depth trade-off that is specific to this size band. Aya-101 \citep{ustun2024aya} is a 13B mT5-based model covering 101 languages, above the compact band; Aya~23 \citep{aryabumi2024aya23} is an 8B and 35B decoder-only pair that allocates the same capacity to 23 languages instead, and its 8B variant outperforms the 13B Aya-101 on the languages both cover. Qwen2.5 7B \citep{qwen2024qwen25} is the most widely downloaded open-weight \gls{slm} for multilingual deployments, with documented coverage of Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic, and the major European languages.
\end{description}

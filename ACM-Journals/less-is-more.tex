\begin{abstract}
  Large language models (LLMs) have transformed natural language processing, but
  their cost, memory footprint, and latency are a poor match for the
  fastest-growing consumer of language intelligence: the AI agent, which issues
  many narrow, repetitive, well-specified model calls rather than a few
  open-ended ones. Small language models (SLMs)---decoder-only or hybrid
  transformers of roughly twenty billion parameters or fewer, counting active
  parameters for mixture-of-experts models---have closed much of the capability
  gap through knowledge distillation, synthetic-data curation, and post-training
  quantization. This article is both a survey and a position statement. We
  catalog 126 documented SLM variants (2022--2026), including the 2025--2026
  hybrid state-space, unified-reasoning, and mobile-first multimodal families,
  and organize them along a four-dimensional taxonomy of \emph{architecture},
  \emph{training strategy}, \emph{modality}, and \emph{application domain}. We
  review compression for the sub-10B regime, where quantization consistently
  outperforms pruning. We then audit the public evaluation record for an 18-model
  cohort and report a negative result that bears on how the field compares these
  models: a cross-model accuracy table cannot be assembled from current public
  reporting without misrepresenting its sources, because vendors report
  non-intersecting benchmark sets, shot counts that differ per benchmark and per
  vendor, base and instruction-tuned figures that are routinely conflated, and few
  matched baselines. On the evidence that does hold we argue that SLMs are the
  substrate of agentic AI: capable enough for
  the sub-tasks agents delegate, roughly 10--30$\times$ cheaper to serve, cheaper
  to specialize, and easier to constrain to tool interfaces. We survey
  heterogeneous SLM--LLM routing and the LLM-to-SLM conversion pipeline, and close
  with the open problems---reasoning under parameter constraints, hallucination,
  hardware co-design, fairness under compression, and empirical verification of
  the consolidated evidence---that stand between the present and that future.
\end{abstract}

\section{Discussion}
\label{section:discussion}

\subsection{What the Evidence Supports}
\label{subsec:discussion_key_findings}

Four conclusions follow from the preceding sections, each at the grade stated.

\textbf{Capability per parameter has risen, and training data is the main lever.} The strongest 3--4B models of 2024--2025 report scores that 13B-class models of 2023 did not report, an across-source comparison whose direction alone we assert (\evD); a single-harness benchmark of 72 small models finds that reasoning tracks training method and data quality more than scale \citep{srivastava2025thinkslm} (\evB).

\textbf{To compress an existing small model, quantize first.} Post-training quantization preserves fidelity better than post-training pruning at 0.5--3.8B (\evA), 4-bit weights are $2.8$--$3.6\times$ smaller than FP16, and the decode speedup that follows is real only in single-stream, bandwidth-bound use, as Meta's measured $2.6\times$ on a phone illustrates (Section~\ref{subsec:deployment_calc_vs_meas}). A single accuracy-retention figure per technique is not supported (Section~\ref{subsec:compression_reporting}).

\textbf{The vendor evaluation record cannot support cross-model comparison.} For the 14 cohort models of the four largest vendors, no benchmark is reported for all of them and no stated protocol is shared by more than five (\evB). Comparison tables built from vendor figures, including those in secondary sources, inherit this; single-harness third-party evaluations are the basis on which comparison should rest.

\textbf{Right-sizing is a conditional design rule, not a general one.} For a call type meeting H1--H4, a specialized small model is plausibly the right default: it is cheaper per call, faster once the network hop is counted, available offline, and its errors are caught by the check. That conclusion is graded \evD{}, because the quantity that decides it, cost per completed task against cached, batched, hosted, and frontier alternatives, has not been measured (Section~\ref{subsubsec:agentic_ev_routing}), and Section~\ref{subsec:agentic_counterexamples} names the workloads where it fails.

\subsection{Implications for Agentic Systems}
\label{subsec:discussion_agentic}

Three implications follow for agent builders. Routing must be capability-aware, not size-aware: a ``small model unless hard'' router degrades non-English and safety-sensitive calls silently (Section~\ref{subsec:reported_safety}). The economical unit is often a fine-tuned small model per recurring call type rather than one general \gls{slm}, which is what the conversion pipeline produces. And the check is part of the design, not an implementation detail: without a correctness check (H4b), an \gls{slm}-first system inherits the compounding of per-call errors that the cascade was meant to stop.

\subsection{Domain Applications}
\label{sec:applications}
\label{subsec:discussion_applications}

The domains where \gls{slm} deployment is most advanced are each a workflow of bounded, repeated language operations under regulatory or cost constraints, and \suppsec~\ref{supp:applications} reviews them with the state of the evidence for each. Healthcare is the best evidenced: Meerkat-7B reports 70.6\% on MedQA \citep{kim2025meerkat} and compressed clinical encoders lose under 5\% in macro-AUROC \citep{hasan2024optimclm} (\evC), but exam benchmarks are not deployment evidence, and on-premises hosting removes one data-transfer question without satisfying HIPAA or the GDPR. Legal NLP has public benchmarks, including LegalBench \citep{guha2023legalbench}, LexGLUE \citep{chalkidis2022lexglue}, and CUAD \citep{hendrycks2021cuad}, but we found no evaluation of current sub-10B decoder models across them under one protocol, so its case for small on-premises models is structural rather than evidential. Code has mature small models evaluated on public benchmarks \citep{roziere2023codellama,lozhkov2024starcoder2,zhu2024deepseek_coder}, and multilingual coverage trades breadth against depth at this size \citep{aryabumi2024aya23}. In every vertical, deployment interest runs ahead of the evidence.

\subsection{Access and Sustainability}
\label{subsec:discussion_implications}

Running a capable 3B model on inexpensive hardware, with no per-query charge and, within the scope conditions of Section~\ref{subsec:deployment_privacy_scope}, no transfer of prompt content, widens access for organizations facing cloud cost, data-sovereignty rules, or unreliable connectivity. The environmental case is narrower than usually stated. Per-query compute scales with active parameters, but per-query energy also depends on utilization, and a well-batched server can be more efficient per token than an idle device; aggregate effects depend further on teacher training, device manufacture, grid intensity, update distribution, and rebound. We therefore make no aggregate energy claim (\suppsec~\ref{supp:energy}).

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:discussion_limitations}

\emph{No original measurement.} No benchmark was executed for this survey; the one grade-\evA{} claim rests on cited controlled studies, and Section~\ref{subsec:challenges_verification} is written to be acted on.

\emph{Inventory provenance.} The inventory began from secondary listings, and 205 records were first compiled with the assistance of a language model. Every included record now links a developer source checked on 8~October 2026, but coverage of models no listing surfaced, especially from smaller developers, is not guaranteed, and the change of seed source in 2026 affects year-to-year comparisons.

\emph{Literature search.} The technique literature was not screened under a recorded protocol, so statements that no study exists are bounded by the search described in Section~\ref{subsec:intro_method}.

\emph{Definitional conventions.} The twenty-billion threshold, the C1/C2 split, and the sub-10B cohort are conventions; Section~\ref{subsubsec:intro_def_operative} gives the counts under other thresholds.

\emph{Scope of H's evidence.} Every agent-specific result concerns models below 10B, and H relies on C1 more than C2: for server-side calls a small-active-parameter \gls{moe} has small-model per-call cost, so it is a comparator in the test of H rather than part of H.

\emph{Coverage.} The benchmarks audited and most of the compression literature are English-centric, the audit covers 2023--2025 releases, and the snapshot ages; the update policy of \suppsec~\ref{subsubsec:intro_snapshot} bounds rather than removes this.

\subsection{Ethical Considerations}
\label{subsec:discussion_ethics}

Alignment is harder to preserve at small scale, several research-grade \glspl{slm} ship without safety training, and fine-tuning can erode what exists \citep{qi2023finetuning}; deployments that give small models tool access should treat output filtering and monitoring as mandatory. Compression can degrade performance unevenly across languages and dialects \citep{blodgett2020language}, and pruning more than quantization \citep{kurz2025pruning_vs_quant}, so high-stakes applications need per-group performance floors. Against these risks, on-device \glspl{slm} remove one egress channel, work without connectivity, and need no subscription, which matters most in under-resourced healthcare and education settings.

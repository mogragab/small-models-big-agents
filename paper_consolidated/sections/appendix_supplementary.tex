This appendix supports the reproducibility of the \emph{review itself}. It records where the quantitative material in Sections~\ref{sec:reported} and~\ref{sec:deployment} comes from, and documents the evaluation and compression conventions the reviewed sources report, so that a reader can judge any individual figure against the conditions behind it. Device specifications are in Table~\ref{tab:appendix_hardware}. The appendix reports no original experimental runs.

\subsection{Provenance Status by Table}
\label{subsec:appendix_provenance_status}

Every quantitative table retained in this paper falls into one of three
categories, and the category determines how much weight it can carry.

\emph{Sourced from primary release documents.} The architectural specifications
in Table~\ref{tab:model_specs} are taken from the release document listed for
each model in Table~\ref{tab:provenance_models}. Layer counts, hidden sizes,
attention configurations, context lengths, vocabulary sizes and training-token
counts are stated directly in those documents and are stable across printings.

\emph{Computed from stated quantities.} The weight footprints in
Table~\ref{tab:memory_footprint} follow arithmetically from parameter count and
an effective number of bits per weight (two bytes per parameter for FP16, 4.4--5.8
effective bits for the four-bit schemes, as stated in its caption) and are
estimates, not measurements. The device specifications in
Table~\ref{tab:hardware_configs} and Table~\ref{tab:appendix_hardware} come from
manufacturer datasheets.

\emph{Structural properties of a technique.} Table~\ref{tab:compression_axes}
states what each compression technique reduces by construction. Its entries are
consequences of how the technique works, not measurements: the memory factor of
$n$-bit quantization follows from the bit-width, and the parameter factor of
$s$-fraction pruning follows from the sparsity level. Where a cell reports a
figure from a specific study, that study is cited in the cell.

\emph{Inventory-derived.} The counts in the text, Table~\ref{tab:reporting_dimensions}, the inventory appendix, and every generated figure are computed from version~3.6 of the released inventory by the released scripts.

\emph{Transcribed coverage.} The coverage ledger (Table~\ref{tab:audit_ledger}) records whether, and under which stated protocol, each model's primary release document or official model card reports each benchmark; the document and table for every cell are listed in \file{audit\_coverage\_ledger.csv}. For the Phi models these are the official model cards \citep{microsoft2025phi4minicard}, and for Llama~3.2 the official model card \citep{meta2024llama32card}.

\emph{Qualitative synthesis of the reviewed literature.} The quality-cost
statements in Table~\ref{tab:compression_quality}, the framework comparison in
Table~\ref{tab:deployment_frameworks}, the survey comparison in
Table~\ref{tab:survey_compare}, the taxonomy summary in
Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy_summary}, and the agent-evidence summary in
Table~\ref{tab:agentic_evidence} summarize the works cited in their respective
sections. They are positional rather than metric, and each row of the two graded
tables carries the class of evidence standing behind it
(Section~\ref{subsec:reported_grading}).

\emph{Not reported by design.} No table in this paper gives a single compression
ratio or accuracy-retention percentage per technique, for the reasons in
Section~\ref{subsec:compression_reporting}, and no table reports a cross-model
accuracy comparison, because, as Section~\ref{subsec:reported_overall} and
Table~\ref{tab:audit_ledger} show, such a table cannot be assembled from vendor
reporting without mixing incompatible protocols and separately reported
checkpoints. A partial table from single-harness third-party evaluations is the
defensible alternative.

\subsection{Prompt Formats in the Source Literature}
\label{subsec:appendix_prompt_templates}
This subsection describes the conventions the reviewed sources use; it does not
describe a protocol applied in this review, which ran no evaluations. The sources
commonly preserve each model's own instruction wrapper while following a broadly
similar task-payload structure. \textbf{Multiple-choice question answering} presents the question
followed by the enumerated options (A--D) and an ``Answer:'' cue, with the model
expected to emit a single option letter. \textbf{Mathematical reasoning} prepends
an instruction to solve the problem step by step and appends a ``Let's think step
by step'' cue, so that a chain-of-thought precedes the final numeric answer.
\textbf{Extractive question answering} supplies the context passage, then the
question, then an instruction to answer from the context and to respond
``unanswerable'' when the context does not contain the answer. \textbf{Abstractive
summarization} supplies the source article preceded by an instruction to produce a
two- to three-sentence summary. Where a source reports few-shot results, it typically prepends a fixed set of
exemplars of its own choosing, which differs from source to source. Because
sources differ in the exemplars, in the exact wording of these wrappers, and in
answer-extraction heuristics, figures from different sources are not comparable,
which is part of the finding of Section~\ref{subsec:reported_overall}.

\subsection{Quantization and Compression Pipeline}
\label{subsec:appendix_quantization_pipeline}
The compression workflows reported across the reviewed sources are summarized here. The reviewed literature reports five recurring precision or compression settings:
\begin{description}
    \item[FP16 baseline] Standard half-precision inference used as the reference condition.
    \item[GGUF Q4\_K\_M] Mixed-precision 4-bit quantization via \texttt{llama.cpp}, assigning higher precision to the most sensitive tensors.
    \item[AWQ 4-bit] Activation-aware weight quantization with group size 128 and calibration on held-out text sequences.
    \item[GPTQ 4-bit] Layer-wise post-training quantization using second-order curvature approximations.
    \item[bitsandbytes NF4] 4-bit NormalFloat quantization with double quantization, matching the \gls{qlora} training regime.
\end{description}

Some sources also report SparseGPT~\citep{frantar2023sparsegpt}-induced 50\% unstructured sparsity applied before 4-bit quantization. We do not tabulate those figures, because support for sparse inference remains hardware dependent and the setting is not uniformly reported across the reviewed sources.

\subsection{Human-Evaluation Conventions in the Source Literature}
\label{subsec:appendix_human_eval}
For subjective tasks such as summarization quality and instruction following, the reviewed sources that report human judgments typically rate outputs along three axes (\textbf{relevance}, \textbf{coherence}, and \textbf{factual accuracy}) on a 1--5 Likert scale, with model identity hidden from annotators and inter-annotator reliability reported via a Krippendorff-style coefficient. MT-Bench-style results substitute a strong \gls{llm} judge for human raters. Because these protocols differ across sources in rubric wording and judge model, and because per-model coverage is sparse, instruction-following and summarization are not tabulated in this review and are discussed only qualitatively.

\section{Related Work and Research Gaps}
\label{section:background}

\subsection{Evolution from LLMs to SLMs}
\label{subsec:background_evolution}
The modern era of language modeling began with the Transformer architecture \citep{vaswani2017attention}, which replaced recurrent networks with self-attention mechanisms enabling efficient parallel training. BERT \citep{devlin2019bert} demonstrated the power of pre-training on large corpora followed by task-specific fine-tuning, establishing a paradigm that dominated \gls{nlp} from 2018 to 2020. The GPT series \citep{brown2020gpt3,radford2019gpt2,openai2023gpt4} progressively scaled this approach, with GPT-3 (175B parameters) revealing emergent capabilities---such as in-context learning and chain-of-thought reasoning---that appeared to arise only at sufficient scale. This observation motivated an aggressive scaling race: PaLM (540B) \citep{chowdhery2023palm}, Chinchilla (70B) \citep{hoffmann2022chinchilla}, LLaMA (7B--65B) \citep{touvron2023llama}, LLaMA-2 (7B--70B) \citep{touvron2023llama2}, Falcon (7B--180B) \citep{almazrouei2023falcon}, and ultimately GPT-4 \citep{openai2023gpt4} and Gemini Ultra \citep{gemini2023} pushed parameter counts into the hundreds of billions. \\

However, the scaling paradigm encountered critical challenges. \citet{kaplan2020scaling} established power-law relationships between model performance and three factors: parameter count, training data size, and compute budget. Crucially, \citet{hoffmann2022chinchilla} revised these scaling laws with the Chinchilla study, demonstrating that for a fixed compute budget, models and training data should be scaled in equal proportion. Their 70B-parameter Chinchilla model, trained on 1.4 trillion tokens, outperformed the 280B-parameter Gopher model trained on only 300 billion tokens. This finding implied that most existing \glspl{llm} were significantly under-trained and that smaller, better-trained models could achieve equivalent performance at lower cost. Recent work has further validated and refined this principle: the Qwen2.5 series trains its 7B model on 18 trillion tokens \citep{qwen2024qwen25}---over 2,500$\times$ the parameter count in tokens---far exceeding the Chinchilla-optimal ratio and demonstrating that the benefits of extended training persist well beyond theoretical optima. \\

The counter-trend toward smaller models accelerated in 2023--2024 with the release of several pivotal \gls{slm} families. Microsoft's Phi-1 (1.3B) \citep{gunasekar2023phi1} demonstrated that a small model trained on carefully curated ``textbook-quality'' data could achieve surprisingly strong performance on code generation tasks. Phi-2 (2.7B) \citep{microsoft2023phi2} extended this to general language understanding, matching or exceeding models with $25\times$ more parameters on certain benchmarks. The Phi-3 family \citep{abdin2024phi3} formalized the synthetic-data-first training philosophy, with Phi-3-mini (3.8B) achieving performance comparable to Mixtral $8\times7$B (46.7B total parameters) on \gls{mmlu}. Google's Gemma \citep{gemma2024} and Gemma 2 \citep{gemma2team2024} leveraged distillation from Gemini models, while Meta's LLaMA-3.2 \citep{grattafiori2024llama3} introduced 1B and 3B variants specifically designed for edge deployment. By early 2026, the Phi-4-mini (3.8B) achieves 88.6\% on \gls{gsm8k} mathematical reasoning \citep{microsoft2025phi4mini}, a score that would have placed it among the top models at any parameter count in 2023.


\subsection{Knowledge Distillation and Model Compression}
\label{subsec:background_distillation}
Knowledge distillation, first formalized by \citet{hinton2015distilling}, transfers knowledge from a large ``teacher'' model to a smaller ``student'' by training the student to match the teacher's soft probability distributions (logits) rather than hard labels. This approach preserves the teacher's learned representations of inter-class similarities---information that is lost in one-hot labels---and has proven remarkably effective in the \gls{llm} era. Modern distillation strategies for language models encompass several complementary approaches. \\

Logit-based distillation trains the student to minimize the KL divergence between its output distribution and the teacher's softened distribution, parameterized by a temperature $\tau$. Google's Gemma 2 \citep{gemma2team2024} and Gemma 3 \citep{team2025gemma3} employ this approach extensively: all Gemma 3 model sizes, including the 27B variant, are trained using knowledge distillation from a larger teacher. The distillation loss is combined with a standard next-token prediction loss, with the relative weighting serving as a key hyperparameter. Feature-based distillation \citep{romero2015fitnets} additionally aligns intermediate representations between teacher and student, capturing structural knowledge that may not be fully expressed in output logits. Microsoft's Orca 2 \citep{mitra2023orca2} demonstrated a variant where a smaller model is taught to use different reasoning strategies depending on task complexity, outperforming models with 5--$10\times$ more parameters on reasoning benchmarks. \\

Synthetic data generation represents an increasingly dominant form of implicit distillation. Rather than direct logit matching, a powerful \gls{llm} (e.g., GPT-4, Claude 3.5) generates high-quality training examples that are then used to train smaller models. The Phi series pioneered this approach with the ``Textbooks Are All You Need'' methodology \citep{gunasekar2023phi1}, generating synthetic textbook-style content with carefully controlled complexity and diversity. Phi-4's training data comprises approximately 40\% synthetic data generated across 50 distinct dataset types, including multi-agent conversations, structured reasoning chains, and domain-specific problem sets \citep{abdin2024phi4}. This synthetic-data-first approach has proven more effective than distillation from raw web corpora, particularly for STEM and reasoning tasks. \\

Post-training compression techniques enable further size reduction without retraining. Quantization reduces the numerical precision of model weights from 32-bit or 16-bit floating point to lower bit-widths. GPTQ \citep{frantar2023gptq} performs layer-wise quantization using approximate second-order (Hessian) information, achieving INT4 precision with minimal perplexity increase. \gls{awq} \citep{lin2024awq} identifies and protects the small fraction (${\sim}1\%$) of salient weights determined by activation magnitudes, achieving near-lossless INT4 quantization and winning the MLSys 2024 Best Paper award. \gls{gguf}, developed for the llama.cpp ecosystem \citep{gerganov2023llamacpp}, provides block-based K-quant schemes (Q4\_K\_M, Q5\_K\_M, Q8\_0) optimized for CPU inference with fine-grained quality--size trade-offs. SqueezeLLM \citep{kim2024squeezellm} introduced a sensitivity-based non-uniform quantization scheme with dense-and-sparse decomposition, demonstrating that sensitivity varies dramatically across weight dimensions. \\

Complementary compression techniques include structured and unstructured pruning, which remove redundant weights or entire attention heads \citep{frantar2023sparsegpt}; \gls{lora} \citep{hu2022lora} and its 4-bit variant \gls{qlora} \citep{dettmers2023qlora}, which enable parameter-efficient fine-tuning by learning low-rank update matrices while keeping the base model frozen; and vocabulary pruning, which reduces embedding table size for domain-specific deployments. The combination of distillation during training, quantization during deployment, and LoRA-based adaptation for task specialization forms the modern \gls{slm} optimization pipeline.

\subsection{Efficient Fine-Tuning Methods}
\label{subsec:background_efficient_finetuning}
Full fine-tuning updates all model parameters using task-specific data, which for a 7B-parameter model can require tens of gigabytes of accelerator memory once gradients and optimizer states are included. This cost has made \gls{peft} central to the practical adaptation of \glspl{slm}, particularly when the target deployment environment is a single GPU or an edge-oriented workstation. \\

\gls{lora} \citep{hu2022lora} addresses this constraint by freezing the pretrained backbone and learning low-rank update matrices for selected linear layers. For a weight matrix $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$, the update is parameterized as $\Delta W = BA$, where $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ and $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$ with rank $r \ll \min(d,k)$. This drastically reduces the number of trainable parameters while preserving the ability to specialize a model for downstream tasks. \gls{qlora} \citep{dettmers2023qlora} extends this idea by quantizing the frozen base model to 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) precision and training the adapters in higher precision, allowing models that would ordinarily require multi-GPU setups to be fine-tuned on a single high-memory accelerator. \\

Beyond LoRA-style adapters, the \gls{peft} landscape includes prompt-based and prefix-based adaptation strategies that shift task information into learned soft prompts or attention prefixes rather than updating the backbone itself. These approaches can be attractive when multiple lightweight task specializations must be maintained simultaneously. However, they tend to underperform LoRA-family methods on complex reasoning tasks, where direct adaptation of attention and feed-forward projections provides a stronger inductive bias for task transfer. \\

The choice between prompting and fine-tuning therefore depends on task specificity, data availability, and deployment constraints. In-context learning offers zero-training adaptation and is well suited to broad tasks when labeled data are scarce, but it consumes context length and raises inference latency. By contrast, \gls{peft} methods incur an upfront training cost yet reduce per-query overhead and typically yield larger gains on narrow or reasoning-heavy tasks. This trade-off motivates the comparative synthesis in Section~\ref{sec:reported}, where reported prompting and \gls{qlora} results are placed side by side.


\subsection{Overview of Key SLM Families}
\label{subsec:background_families}

\subsubsection{Microsoft Phi Series}
\label{subsubsec:phi_series}
The Phi series represents Microsoft's systematic exploration of data-quality-driven model development. Phi-1 (1.3B) \citep{gunasekar2023phi1} demonstrated strong code generation performance through training on synthetic ``textbook-quality'' data. Phi-2 (2.7B) \citep{microsoft2023phi2} extended this to general language understanding. Phi-3-mini (3.8B) \citep{abdin2024phi3} formalized the approach with 3.3 trillion training tokens, achieving 68.8\% on \gls{mmlu} and 82.5\% on \gls{gsm8k} with instruction tuning. Phi-3.5-mini \citep{abdin2024phi35} added 128K context length support with improved multilingual capabilities. Phi-4 (14B) \citep{abdin2024phi4} introduced a fundamentally enhanced training pipeline incorporating ${\sim}40\%$ synthetic data, achieving 84.8\% on \gls{mmlu} and 80.4\% on MATH---surpassing its teacher model GPT-4o on several STEM benchmarks. Phi-4-mini (3.8B) \citep{microsoft2025phi4mini} updated the architecture with \gls{gqa} (24 query, 8 \gls{kv} heads), a 200K-token vocabulary, and training on 5 trillion tokens, achieving 88.6\% on \gls{gsm8k}. The Phi-4-reasoning variant \citep{abdin2025phi4reasoning} applied supervised fine-tuning on o3-mini reasoning demonstrations followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), reaching 82.5\% on AIME 2025---competitive with DeepSeek-R1 at 671B parameters.

\subsubsection{Google Gemma Series}
\label{subsubsec:gemma_series}
Google's Gemma family \citep{gemma2024} derives from the Gemini model architecture and training infrastructure. Gemma 1 offered 2B and 7B variants trained on 3T and 6T tokens respectively, using Multi-Query Attention (MQA) for the 2B model and standard Multi-Head Attention for the 7B. Gemma 2 \citep{gemma2team2024} introduced significant architectural improvements: local-global interleaved attention layers, logit soft-capping, and knowledge distillation for the 2B and 9B variants (from a larger teacher model). Gemma 2-9B achieved 71.3\% on \gls{mmlu} (5-shot), establishing it as a strong 7B-class competitor. Gemma 3 \citep{team2025gemma3} further advanced the architecture with a 5:1 local-to-global attention interleaving ratio (reducing the sliding window from 4096 to 1024 tokens), Rotary Position Embeddings, and logit soft-capping at 30.0 for attention and 50.0 for the final layer. Critically, Gemma 3 distills all model sizes from larger teachers and introduces a 1B variant that achieves 48.0\% on MATH---nearly matching Gemma 2-9B's 49.4\% at $9\times$ fewer parameters.

\subsubsection{Alibaba Qwen Series}
\label{subsubsec:qwen_series}
The Qwen2 \citep{yang2024qwen2} and Qwen2.5 \citep{qwen2024qwen25} families from Alibaba represent the most extensively scaled \gls{slm} training effort, with models trained on 18 trillion tokens---substantially exceeding Chinchilla-optimal ratios. Qwen2.5 offers models at 0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B, 32B, and 72B parameters, all sharing a consistent GQA-based architecture with \gls{rope} and SwiGLU activations. The 3B variant achieves remarkable results: 65.6\% on \gls{mmlu}, 79.1\% on \gls{gsm8k}, and 42.6\% on MATH---scores that establish it as arguably the strongest sub-4B base model available. Qwen2.5's training data includes substantial STEM and code content, explaining its particular strength on mathematical and programming benchmarks.

\subsubsection{Other Significant SLM Families}
\label{subsubsec:other_slm_families}
TinyLlama (1.1B) \citep{zhang2024tinyllama} adopted the LLaMA-2 architecture and trained on 3 trillion tokens from SlimPajama and StarCoder data, demonstrating that extended training of very small models yields disproportionate gains. Mistral 7B \citep{jiang2023mistral} introduced Grouped-Query Attention and Sliding Window Attention in a 7.3B-parameter model, achieving performance competitive with LLaMA-2 13B while being $1.8\times$ smaller. Meta's Llama 3.2 \citep{grattafiori2024llama3} released dedicated 1B and 3B models trained on 9 trillion tokens with 128K context length, specifically targeting edge deployment. SmolLM2 (135M, 360M, 1.7B) \citep{allal2025smollm2} from Hugging Face trained on 11 trillion tokens of carefully curated data. H2O-Danube (1.8B) \citep{singer2024h2odanube} introduced sliding window attention in a compact model trained on 1 trillion tokens. OLMo 2 (1B, 7B, 13B) \citep{olmo2025} from AI2 was released as a fully open model (data, code, weights, and training logs), achieving competitive performance through QK-norm and carefully staged training. StableLM-2 (1.6B) \citep{stabilityai2024stablelm} was trained on 4 trillion tokens of multilingual data. The BLOOM family (560M, 1.1B, 1.7B, 3B, 7.1B) \citep{bloom2022} remains notable as a multilingual model trained on 46 languages using ALiBi positional encoding, though its 350B-token training budget is modest by current standards.


\subsection{Edge AI and On-Device Inference}
\label{subsec:background_edge_ai}
The edge AI inference stack has matured rapidly, transforming \gls{slm} deployment from a research exercise to a production-ready capability. The llama.cpp framework \citep{gerganov2023llamacpp} remains the de facto standard for CPU and mixed CPU-GPU inference, supporting the \gls{gguf} quantization format with dozens of precision variants (Q2\_K through Q8\_0). Its ecosystem includes llamafile (single-executable packaging), Ollama (user-friendly serving), and extensive platform support spanning x86, ARM, Apple Silicon, and NVIDIA GPUs. Benchmarking on single-board computers shows that llamafile achieves up to $4\times$ higher throughput and 30--40\% lower power consumption than Ollama for equivalent models \citep{costa2025sbc}. \\

Meta's ExecuTorch \citep{meta2024executorch} reached version 1.0 GA in October 2025, providing a production-ready framework for on-device AI with a 50~KB base runtime and support for 12+ hardware backends including CoreML, Qualcomm QNN, ARM, MediaTek, and Vulkan. It is deployed in production across Instagram, WhatsApp, and Facebook, serving billions of users. MLC-LLM \citep{mlc2024} provides universal deployment through ML compilation (TVM), supporting iOS, Android, web, and embedded platforms. ONNX Runtime \citep{microsoft2019onnx} enables cross-platform enterprise deployment with optimization passes for diverse hardware targets. WebLLM \citep{webllm2024} brings browser-based inference using WebGPU acceleration, retaining up to 80\% of native performance with an OpenAI-compatible API. Google's LiteRT (formerly TensorFlow Lite) with QNN acceleration achieves $100\times$ CPU speedup on Qualcomm \glspl{npu} \citep{google2025litert}, enabling sub-second time-to-first-token for sub-1B models on flagship smartphones. \\

The fundamental constraint in edge deployment is memory bandwidth rather than compute. Flagship mobile and laptop devices offer roughly 50--90~GB/s of memory bandwidth, and single-board computers well under 30~GB/s, versus 2--3~TB/s for datacenter GPUs \citep{xu2024device}. Since autoregressive inference is memory-bandwidth-bound (each token generation requires reading all model weights), quantization from 16-bit to 4-bit provides a near-linear $4\times$ speedup by reducing memory transfer volume. This makes INT4 quantization not merely a storage optimization but a critical performance enabler for on-device deployment.


\subsection{LLM--SLM Collaboration and Agentic Systems}
\label{subsec:background_collab}
A distinct line of work studies how small and large models cooperate rather than compete. \citet{chen2024role_small} frame the relationship along two axes---collaboration and competition---and argue that small models remain practically indispensable despite the scaling race. \citet{chen2025collab_survey} organize the collaboration literature into five interaction patterns: pipeline, routing, auxiliary, distillation, and fusion. Routing is the most directly relevant to agents: RouteLLM \citep{ong2024routellm} learns from preference data to send each query to a weak or strong model, reporting more than 2$\times$ cost reduction at matched quality, and Division-of-Thoughts \citep{division_of_thoughts_2025} routes decomposed sub-questions between an on-device \gls{slm} and a cloud \gls{llm}. Surveys of edge--cloud collaborative inference \citep{xu2024device} catalog the systems techniques---speculative decoding, cascades, KV-cache offload---that make the hybrid practical. On the agentic side specifically, \citet{belcak2025slm_agentic} argue that \glspl{slm} should be the default model inside agents, and action-model families such as xLAM \citep{zhang2024xlam} show that sub-10B models can top function-calling leaderboards. This paper connects that argument to a consolidated capability-and-deployment review (Sections~\ref{sec:reported}--\ref{sec:agentic}).

\subsection{Gaps in Existing Literature}
\label{subsec:background_gaps}
Several \gls{slm} surveys appeared in 2024--2025. \citet{lu2024slmsurvey} analyze 70 models on architecture, capability, and on-device runtime, but as a measurement study rather than a synthesis of the wider reported record. \citet{vannguyen2024slm_survey} and \citet{wang2024comprehensive} provide broad technique taxonomies and application coverage. All three predate the 2025--2026 wave of hybrid state-space, unified-reasoning, and mobile-multimodal models, and none foreground agentic deployment. Position pieces on \glspl{slm} in agents \citep{belcak2025slm_agentic} make the forward-looking argument but do not ground it in a consolidated capability review. This paper addresses four gaps: (1)~a landscape and taxonomy current to 2026, including hybrid and reasoning families; (2)~a single consolidated view of \emph{reported} accuracy \emph{and} deployment behavior across many models, tiers, and precisions, with per-figure provenance; (3)~an explicit bridge from that evidence to the agentic case; and (4)~a practitioner-facing decision and verification agenda.

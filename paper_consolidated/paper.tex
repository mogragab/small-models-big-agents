  \begin{abstract}
  Large Language Models (LLMs) with hundreds of billions of parameters
  have advanced natural language processing dramatically, but their
  computational cost, memory demand, and latency make them a poor fit for
  the fastest-growing consumer of language intelligence: the AI agent,
  which issues many narrow, repetitive, well-specified model calls rather
  than a few open-ended ones. Small Language Models
  (SLMs)---here, decoder-only or hybrid transformers with roughly twenty
  billion parameters or fewer, counting active parameters for
  mixture-of-experts models---have closed much of the capability gap
  through knowledge distillation, synthetic-data curation, and
  post-training quantization, and are now the natural default for agentic
  workloads. This paper is a combined survey and position statement. We
  catalog 123 publicly documented SLM variants released between 2022 and 2026,
  including the 2025--2026 wave of hybrid state-space models (Falcon-H1,
  IBM Granite~4.0, NVIDIA Nemotron Nano~2), unified reasoning models
  (Qwen3, Phi-4-mini-reasoning, SmolLM3), and mobile-first multimodal
  families (Gemma~3n, Gemma~4, Apple Foundation Models). We organize the
  landscape along a four-dimensional taxonomy---\emph{architecture},
  \emph{training strategy}, \emph{modality}, and \emph{application
  domain}---and review the compression-technique literature specific to
  the sub-10B regime, in which quantization consistently outperforms
  pruning. We then consolidate publicly \emph{reported} performance for a
  17-model cohort across 15 benchmark tasks, three evaluation paradigms,
  six hardware tiers, and three quantization precisions, under a
  documented grey-literature protocol; every figure is a reported value
  traced to a dated source rather than an original benchmark run. Building
  on this evidence, we argue that SLMs are the substrate of agentic AI:
  they meet the capability threshold for the sub-tasks agents actually
  delegate, they cost 10--30$\times$ less to serve, they are cheaper to
  specialize, and their behavior is easier to constrain to tool and code
  interfaces. We survey heterogeneous SLM--LLM architectures, learned
  routing, and the LLM-to-SLM conversion pipeline, and close with the
  open problems---reasoning under parameter constraints, hallucination,
  hardware co-design, fairness under compression, and empirical
  verification of the consolidated evidence base---that stand between the
  current state of the art and that future.
  \end{abstract}

  \begin{highlights}
  \item Surveys 123 documented small language model variants (2022--2026), including the 2025--2026 hybrid state-space, unified-reasoning, and mobile-multimodal waves, under a taxonomy of architecture, training strategy, modality, and application domain.
  \item Consolidates publicly reported performance for 17 SLMs across 15 tasks, six hardware tiers, and FP16/INT8/INT4 precision, with every figure traced to a dated source via a documented protocol.
  \item Reviews compression for the sub-10B regime and the finding that quantization consistently outperforms pruning below 4B parameters.
  \item Argues that SLMs are the substrate of agentic AI on grounds of capability sufficiency, 10--30$\times$ lower serving cost, cheaper specialization, and tighter behavioral alignment to tool interfaces.
  \item Surveys heterogeneous SLM--LLM routing and the LLM-to-SLM conversion pipeline, and sets an empirical-verification agenda for the consolidated claims.
  \end{highlights}

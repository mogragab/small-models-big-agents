\section{Model Compression Techniques for SLMs}
\label{sec:compression}

Distillation, quantization, and pruning are the principal routes from a large or full-precision model to one deployable on edge hardware, and compound pipelines combine them. This section states how their results are reported here, summarizes what each technique delivers, and grades what the literature establishes about its quality cost. \suppsec~\ref{supp:compression} and~\ref{supp:kd} review the individual methods.

\subsection{How Compression Results Are Reported Here}
\label{subsec:compression_reporting}

Compression results are routinely summarized as one ``compression ratio'' against one ``accuracy retention'' percentage. That summary does not survive scrutiny. \emph{There is no single compression ratio}: quantization reduces bits per weight and leaves the parameter count untouched; pruning reduces the parameter count, and memory only where the runtime exploits the sparsity pattern; distillation reduces the training compute needed to reach a given quality, while the student's size is the practitioner's choice, so a ``10--100$\times$'' distillation ratio describes design choices, not a property of the method. \emph{There is no baseline-free retention}: a figure such as ``about 96\%'' is uninterpretable without the baseline precision, model, size, task, metric, and aggregation rule, each of which changes it materially \citep{lin2024awq,srivastava2025thinkslm}. Table~\ref{tab:compression_axes} therefore separates what each technique reduces along four axes that are not interchangeable and are not summed, and Table~\ref{tab:compression_quality} states what the literature establishes about quality cost as a direction with its scope and grade. Where no study supports a number, none is given, and Section~\ref{subsec:challenges_verification} specifies the measurement that would.

\subsection{Distillation, Quantization, and Pruning}
\label{subsec:compression_kd}
\label{subsec:compression_quantization}
\label{subsec:compression_pruning}

\emph{Knowledge distillation} \citep{hinton2015distilling} trains a student to match a teacher's output distribution, its intermediate representations (white-box), or its generated text and reasoning traces (black-box), as in Orca \citep{mukherjee2023orca} and the DeepSeek-R1 students \citep{deepseek2025r1}; synthetic-data training, the mechanism behind Phi \citep{gunasekar2023phi1}, is distillation through the data. \emph{Quantization} reduces numerical precision. Post-training methods need only a small calibration set: GPTQ \citep{frantar2023gptq} uses second-order information, \gls{awq} \citep{lin2024awq} protects activation-salient channels, and LLM.int8() and SmoothQuant handle activation outliers at 8 bits \citep{dettmers2022llmint8,xiao2023smoothquant}. \gls{gguf} \citep{gerganov2023llamacpp} is the format in which most sub-4B models are distributed for CPU deployment. Quantization-aware training recovers part of what post-training quantization loses at 2--3 bits but needs the training stack, which is why it appears mainly in vendor pipelines \citep{gunter2025afm}, and its limit case, ternary weights trained from scratch \citep{ma2024bitnet158}, is a training recipe rather than a compression of an existing model. \emph{Pruning} removes individual weights (SparseGPT \citep{frantar2023sparsegpt}, Wanda \citep{sun2024wanda}) or whole structures (LLM-Pruner \citep{ma2023llmpruner}, ShortGPT \citep{men2024shortgpt}); structured pruning followed by continued training is a cheaper route to a small model than training from scratch \citep{xia2024sheared}.

The one controlled comparison in the sub-10B regime concerns the choice between the first two kinds of post-training compression. \citet{kurz2025pruning_vs_quant} compare GPTQ and \gls{awq} against SparseGPT and Wanda on six models of 0.5--3.8B across seven languages and seven tasks, and find that quantization preserves fidelity, multilingual perplexity, and reasoning accuracy better than pruning, with the advantage shrinking on knowledge-and-reasoning tasks such as OpenBookQA and with Wanda's parity with SparseGPT at large scale not holding at this size; \citet{srivastava2025thinkslm}, across 72 small models, likewise find that quantization preserves reasoning while pruning disrupts it. This is the one grade-\evA{} claim in the paper. Its scope is post-training methods at their usual operating points, 4-bit weights against 50\% sparsity, which are not equal reductions: 4-bit weights cut memory by about $3\times$ on any runtime, while 50\% unstructured sparsity saves memory only on runtimes that exploit it. The practical reading is that to compress an existing small model after training, quantize first; structured pruning followed by distillation, which derives a new model, is outside the comparison.

\subsection{Combined Pipelines}
\label{subsec:compression_combined}

The strongest reported results combine stages. Minitron \citep{minitron2024} prunes Nemotron-4 15B in depth and width and retrains the pruned students by distillation from the original on a small fraction of its data; deriving the 8B and 4B models this way needs up to $40\times$ fewer training tokens per model than training from scratch, a $1.8\times$ compute saving for the family, and the derived models are reported to compare with similarly sized community models (\evC). A follow-up applies the recipe to Llama~3.1 8B and Mistral NeMo 12B \citep{sreenivas2024llmpruning}, and Meta derived Llama~3.2 1B and 3B by pruning Llama~3.1 8B and distilling from the 8B and 70B models \citep{meta2024llama32}. Quantization is applied afterwards for deployment and is not part of these recipes. In clinical NLP, OptimCLM \citep{hasan2024optimclm} composes distillation, pruning, and 8-bit quantization of BERT encoders (\evC); it shows that the stages compose, not that they do for generative models.

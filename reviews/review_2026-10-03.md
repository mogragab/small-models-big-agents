# Review: Small Language Models as the Substrate of Agentic AI: A Survey of Architectures, Compression, Deployment, and Evaluation Practice

*Reviewed 2026-10-03 · Mode: full (four lenses run as separate passes, then merged and checked against the manuscript) · Coverage: `ACM-Journals/less-is-more.tex` and every included section, partial and appendix (section files are identical to `paper_consolidated/sections/`), the compiled PDF (74 pp.), `bibliography.bib` (149 entries), `source/merged_llm_database.json` v3.4 and `source/scripts/` · Venue: ACM Computing Surveys*

The four lenses are separate passes of one model, not independent referees. Where several lenses agree, confidence goes up, but that agreement is not proof. Every finding below marked **verified** was re-checked by hand against the manuscript, the data, or the primary source.

## Summary of the submission

The paper makes six moves:

1. It sets a two-condition definition of a small language model: at most about 20B parameters both active per token (C1) and in total (C2).
2. It catalogs 215 models (2022 to 2026) under a faceted taxonomy and releases the inventory as JSON.
3. It reviews compression in the sub-10B regime along separate reduction axes.
4. It audits vendor-reported evaluations for an 18-model cohort and reports a negative result: a cross-model accuracy table cannot honestly be built from the public record.
5. It argues a conditional hypothesis H: for agent calls that are bounded, recurrent, short-context and cheaply verifiable, a specialized SLM should be the default, with a frontier model on escalation.
6. It grades every claim A–D by evidence strength and closes with six proposed verification experiments.

## Overall assessment

**Not ready to submit.** The core intellectual moves are good and unusually self-critical: the C1/C2 split, the A–D grading, and the explicit counterexamples to H. But two problems would each, on their own, sink the paper at the editor or the first referee:

- **The documented survey protocol has no outputs.** The search window, every screening count, the inter-rater agreement and the DOI are still `[TO SUPPLY]`. The released database also records a different provenance (blog aggregators, plus "generated" records) from the one the paper describes.
- **The bibliography contains entries with invented co-author lists and one URL that points to an unrelated paper.** Reviewers read this as a fabrication signal.

Behind those sit a cluster of major issues:

- The headline count does not follow the paper's own rule.
- The single strongest piece of evidence for H is misreported.
- The negative result is stated more broadly than the audit shows.
- The closest prior work is not cited.
- Several technical descriptions misstate their sources.

All of this is fixable, and the conditional framing means the core can survive. As written, though, the advisory outcome is desk rejection or major revision. Fix the two critical items first, because they are cheap to verify and expensive to be caught on.

## Strengths

- **C1/C2 definition.** It separates per-token cost from residency cost. Small-active MoEs are labelled separately and excluded from on-device claims. No prior SLM survey checked does this (§1.2.1–1.2.2).
- **H is stated conditionally.** The counterexample classes are explicit, and planning and uncheckable generation are flagged as not transitional (§1.4, `subsec:agentic_counterexamples`).
- **Honest evidence grading.** No claim is graded A, H itself is graded D, and the decisive missing measurement (cost per completed task) is named, with an experiment specified for it (`tab:claim_grades`, `tab:agentic_evidence`, §10.7).
- **Withdrawals instead of patches.** The single compression ratio, the accuracy-retention bands, the cross-model tables and the energy claim are withdrawn. Calculated and measured quantities are kept apart, and the 4× decode speedup is limited to the regime where it holds (§6.4.1).
- **Counts reproduce from the JSON.** The 215 / 183 / 27 / 5 split, 43 releases in 2026, 210/215 with total parameters, 35/215 with active parameters, and 142/215 with context length all match the data. **Verified.**
- **Correct arithmetic and quotations.** The compounding-error arithmetic and the quoted end-to-end figures (WebArena 14.41/78.24, GAIA 15/92) are correct.
- **Clean build.** `check_latex` finds 0 errors: every citation and `\ref` resolves and every graphic exists.

## Critical and major issues

### 1. The survey protocol is described but its outputs are missing, and the data records a different provenance (critical)

- **Location:**
  - §1.6.2–1.6.4 (`introduction.tex:247-248, 300-303`);
  - `tab:screening` (20 cells are `\needsnum`);
  - Declarations, Data availability (`declarations.tex:41`);
  - `source/merged_llm_database.json` → `metadata`.
- **Issue:**
  - **Unfilled placeholders.** 16 `\needsdata`/`\needsnum` macros are still unfilled (they render as 27 `[TO SUPPLY]` marks). They cover the search window, the last release date, the number of screeners, Cohen's κ, every screening count and the archival DOI.
  - **Seed sources contradict the stated search.** The paper describes a search of the HF Hub, vendor channels, arXiv and forward citations. The database's `metadata.sources` lists 16 seeds, all blog listicles and aggregators (BentoML, Exploding Topics, Zapier, Pluralsight, KDnuggets, Analytics Vidhya, OpenRouter …). `merge_info` records `generated_data_models_added: 205`.
  - **Promised per-record fields are missing.** §1.6.5 says every record carries a source retrieval date and an inclusion decision. Only **28/215** records have `source_retrieved`, and no record has an inclusion-decision field.
  - **Released artifacts don't exist yet.** No screening ledger, source ledger or standalone changelog exists in the repository.
  - **Internal contradiction.** §1.6.2 says the last release date is pending, while §1.6.6 states it as 4 Sept 2026.
- **Why it matters:** CSUR reviewers check whether a stated protocol was actually followed. A PRISMA-style table full of placeholders, next to a dataset whose own metadata names listicles as sources, invites the conclusion that the protocol is retrospective.
- **Evidence:** Verified directly from the JSON and with `git ls-files`.
- **Remedy:**
  - **Option (a), if screening was done:** release the screening ledger, fill `tab:screening`, κ and the dates, add `source_retrieved` and an `inclusion_decision` to every record, and mint the DOI.
  - **Option (b), if screening was not done:** rewrite §1.6 to describe the procedure actually followed (aggregator seeding, then verification against the vendor primary source). Drop the PRISMA flow and κ, and say plainly which records were verified against a primary source.
  - **Either way:** explain what "generated data models" means. If any record or field came from a language model rather than a primary source, re-verify those records and disclose it.
  - Set `\needsdatafinaltrue` for the submission build, so that no placeholder can ship.
- *Raised by lenses:* methods, contribution (also my own data check)

### 4. The headline count does not follow the paper's own rule (major)

    - Nous Hermes 2 Mixtral, Vicuna and TinyLlama-Chat are community fine-tunes, which rule M excludes.

### 5. The strongest evidence for H is misreported, and grades are applied against the scheme's own definitions (major)

- **Location:** §7.3.1 (`agentic.tex:78-85`), §2.5, `tab:agentic_evidence`, `tab:claim_grades`, `tab:compression_quality`.
- **Issue:**
  - **The xLAM claim is wrong.** The paper says xLAM "reports its 7B function-calling model at the top of the leaderboard" and calls this "the strongest single piece of evidence in this paper". The xLAM model card says the 7B model placed **3rd** (88.24%, BFCL as of 07/18/2024) (**verified**: huggingface.co/Salesforce/xLAM-7b-fc-r). The xLAM paper gives 1st place to xLAM-8x22b-r, which at about 141B total is not an SLM under C2. The 7B and 1B comparisons against GPT-3.5 and Claude-3 Haiku come from APIGen (arXiv 2406.18518), which is not cited. The ranking also comes from the vendor, so it is grade C, not B. It is single-turn BFCL v1/v2, and sub-10B standings on the multi-turn and agentic splits are not reported.
  - **Other grades contradict the scheme:**
    - The paper's own audit ("cross-model table cannot be assembled") is graded B, but by §5.1.6 the authors' synthesis is D.
    - Zhou/Kurz/Zhao (one harness, matched models, only the method varied) meets the grade-A definition, yet the paper says nothing reaches A.
    - The constrained-decoding row cites only the method authors (C), but is graded B.
    - "INT8 effectively lossless … widely reproduced" is graded B with no citation.
    - "Derived" appears as a grade outside the A–D scale.
    - Several strong claims are ungraded: DeepSeek-R1-7B "frontier-class reasoning"; "differ by a factor of three in cost ratio"; the "below 3B adaptation stops being optional" claim, which the definition cites to the conclusion and the conclusion cites to nothing.
- **Why it matters:** Grading is the paper's selling point. Inconsistent grades, and a misreported top exhibit, undo it.
- **Remedy:**
  - Re-cite the xLAM claim to APIGen plus a dated, archived BFCL snapshot, reword it to "competitive", grade it C, and add current sub-10B multi-turn BFCL standings.
  - Re-grade every row against the written definitions, or tighten the definition of A.
  - Give every quantitative sentence a citation and a grade.
  - Soften the abstract's "strong at the level of the individual call" to match.

### 7. Novelty: the closest prior work is not cited, and the agentic section's delta over Belcak et al. is not marked (major)

- **Location:** §1.5 ("This paper joins the two"), §2 `subsec:background_gaps`, `tab:survey_compare`, §7.
- **Issue:**

### 8. H is under-operationalized, and the title and conclusion claim more than H (major)

- **Location:** §1.4 (H; "The hypothesis is falsifiable"), §7.2.2 economics, Fig. `hetero_agent` ("most calls"), conclusion paragraphs 2 and 3, title.
- **Issue:**
  - **Circular conditions.** Condition (ii) is defined by its outcome ("often enough to repay one-time specialization"), and condition (iv) removes the main failure mode the paper itself identifies (compounding error).
  - **No decision rule or falsifier.** "Appropriate default" is not tied to any rule, and no result is named that would falsify H.
  - **Economic premise.** It rests on one grade-C per-token estimate against 70–175B models. Hosted small models and small-active MoEs are left out of the comparison, which matters for server-side calls where C2 is irrelevant. The fixed costs (curation, fine-tuning, drift retraining, router calibration) are never sized.
  - **Prevalence of qualifying calls.** No evidence says what share of agent calls satisfy H's conditions, yet the figure, the conclusion ("a large share", "mostly on-device", "less … is the design") and the title assert the strong form.
  - **Numbering clash.** "Premise (iii)" is used both for H's condition (iii) and for the third agentic premise. Conditions (ii) and (iii) have no evidence row at all.
- **Remedy:**
  - Operationalize each condition independently of the outcome: calls per type per day; output-schema cardinality; checker cost relative to call cost.
  - State the decision rule and the result that would falsify H in the §10.7 cost-per-task experiment.
  - Add a total-cost-of-ownership / break-even sketch, and add hosted-small and small-active-MoE arms.
  - Add a "call-type prevalence: none" row to `tab:agentic_evidence`.
  - Rewrite the closing paragraph to restate H, and retitle, e.g. *"Small Language Models for Agentic AI: A Survey of Architectures, Compression, Deployment, and Evaluation, with a Conditional Substrate Hypothesis"*.
  - Use separate labels for H's conditions and the agentic premises.
- *Raised by lenses:* devil's advocate, contribution

### 10. Technical descriptions that misstate their sources (major as a cluster: each one is checkable, and two sit in graded tables)

- **OptimCLM** (`applications.tex:44`, §3, §4.5, `tab:compression_quality`; **verified** text): the paper says "a 7B model to under 1B", 4-bit quantization, structured pruning, ICD coding and summarization. The source actually distills BERT-family clinical encoders into TinyBERT/BERT-PKD with unstructured pruning and 8-bit quantization, evaluated on outcome prediction (macro-AUROC). It says nothing about generative SLMs. Rewrite all four mentions or drop it.
- **Minitron**: the "40× training compute" is really "up to 40× fewer training *tokens*" (Nemotron-4 15B → 8B/4B, arXiv 2407.14679). Llama-3.1-8B → 4B is a separate paper (arXiv 2408.11796). Neither paper uses PTQ as a pipeline step.
- **Gemma 3**: Gemma 3 replaced soft-capping with QK-norm, and the quoted 30/50 values are Gemma 2's, with the two swapped (attention 50, final 30) (`background.tex:45`; **verified** text).
- **Phi-4-reasoning**: the SFT-only model scores about 63% on AIME 2025. GRPO and 78.0% belong to Phi-4-reasoning-plus. No variant reaches 82.5%.
- **Quantization beats pruning**: this is one study of PTQ against post-training pruning at 0.5–3.8B. AWQ-4bit versus 50% Wanda is not "matched compression". The source says the advantage diminishes on reasoning tasks (OpenBookQA). The abstract presents it as the survey's own finding ("finding that…"). Attribute it to the study, drop "matched", and add the caveat. Also add the source's point that Wanda's parity with SparseGPT does not hold for SLMs.
- **SparseGPT**: it ran on OPT-175B and BLOOM-176B, not GPT-3, and it *does* reconstruct the remaining weights.
- *Raised by lenses:* domain, methods
